# M134 Better sound 4

What [M122](m122.md) left out.

- **Effects on cast receivers.** Cast's default receiver, UPnP renderers and FCast take a file, a
  speed and a volume; none can apply voice boost, skip silence or even loudness. The phone could
  play the book through its own effects into an encoder (Media3 and `MediaCodec`, AAC or Opus)
  and serve that as a live stream from its LAN file server (`modules/cast`, `MediaServer.kt`),
  which every receiver plays. Seeking and the place would then be the phone's, not the
  receiver's. The audio stays on the home network, never on a Lelap server.
- **Variable-bitrate MP3.** `vbrMp3Track` (`packages/core/src/webTracks.ts`) counts frames from
  the first and keeps every 64th; its pause map matched a decode of the whole file (27 of 27
  pauses within 10 ms, a LAME `-V 6` copy of LibriVox's The Time Machine, part 1). But Chrome's
  own time after a seek doesn't follow the frames: playing from 1:00, the sound was loud during
  8.4 s of the 10.6 s played fast, and from 15:00, 3.9 s of 6.3 s. So the web player leaves these
  files alone. Chrome probably places a seek by the Xing table of contents; the map would have to
  follow the browser's own seek, measured in Chrome, Firefox and Safari. Counting up to a far
  place also read 6.7 MB.
- **MP3's encoder delay in Chrome.** M101 places MP3 sound by the element's time minus the LAME
  encoder delay. Measured in M122 against the sound actually playing, LibriVox's 64 kbit/s MP3
  went quiet later than the pause map said (median 47 to 68 ms over four runs, against 12 to 16 ms for
  M4B, Opus, Vorbis and FLAC); the file's delay is 50 ms, so Chrome may not trim it.
  The fast stretches still stayed inside the pauses. Whether to drop the delay per browser needs
  the same measurement in Firefox and Safari.
