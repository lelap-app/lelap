# M134 Better sound 4

What [M122](m122.md) left out. Decision in the [brief](../brief.md#decisions) (“Sound effects”).

- **Effects on cast receivers** (`modules/audio`: `EffectsStream.kt`, `CastEffects.kt`;
  `modules/cast`: `LiveAudio.kt`, `MediaServer.kt`, `RemoteSession.kt`). With voice boost, skip
  silence, even loudness, mono or volume boost on, the phone renders each part for the receiver:
  `MediaExtractor` and `MediaCodec` decode the file where it lives (the phone, a WebDAV, SMB or
  SFTP server, an HTTP host with its login sent to that host only), `EffectsPipeline` runs skip
  silence, the effects and speed in the phone player's order, and Android's AAC-LC encoder
  writes ADTS frames (64 kbit/s mono, 96 kbit/s stereo). The LAN file server sends them as a
  stream without a length or ranges (`audio/aac`, DLNA flags for a live stream); the receiver's
  reading paces the phone. The audio stays on the home network. The receiver plays it at 1×; a
  seek, a new speed or skip silence switched on or off starts the stream again at the place,
  while voice boost, even loudness, mono and volume boost change as it plays. `RenderClock`
  maps the receiver's place in the stream back to the part's, from marks the renderer sets every
  200 ms and at each pause it leaves out. Every fetch renders the same stream, so a group's
  speakers all get it; drift seeks are left out for it, and a speaker that joins makes the group
  start it again at the place. A new stream closes the connections of the one before. Without
  effects, receivers get the file as before. Logged as `cast-render` lines (`adb logcat -s
LelapCast`).
- **Variable-bitrate MP3 on the web, in Chrome** (`packages/core/src/webTracks.ts`: `xingSeek`,
  `vbrMp3Track`; `apps/app/src/platform/websilence.ts`, `webfile.ts`). Measured in headless
  Chrome 158 against the sound playing (an analyser after the element, onsets matched to a
  generated 16-minute recording with known word starts, LAME `-V 6` at 44.1 and 22.05 kHz):
  after a seek, Chrome's time counts from the Xing table of contents' byte for the seek's whole
  hundredth, starting with the frame that holds that byte, which gets the hundredth's time. A
  seek before the encoder delay ends keeps the file's start. That rule predicted all 13 seeks to
  within 3 ms, where the frames' own times were off by −1.5 to +3.5 s. The track now counts frames
  from where a seek lands, and the pause map starts again after each seek that moves the
  timeline; end to end, the map's word starts matched the sound within 4 ms after seeks at 1 to 15
  minutes. Firefox 147 estimates the frame from an average frame length after its own table seek,
  1 to 3 frames off in ways that vary by file, so Firefox, Safari and files without a table play
  these files unchanged (`seeksByXingTable`).
- **MP3's encoder delay** (measured, nothing changed). Both browsers leave the LAME delay out at
  the start: from 0, CBR and VBR MP3 played within 2 ms of FLAC, at 25 and 50 ms of delay. So M101
  is right to subtract it. What M122 heard as MP3 going quiet late is where a seek lands: after
  a seek in a CBR file, Chrome played 21 to 50 ms behind the frames' times (different at each
  place) and Firefox one frame (26 ms) ahead. Skip silence's fast stretches begin 150 ms into a
  pause and end 130 ms before the voice, so that stays inside the pauses.
