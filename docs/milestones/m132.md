# M132 Open lectures

Free university lecture courses as books: one course is one book, one lecture one part. It starts
with Open Yale Courses, from a hand-kept list of complete courses. One of Lelap's own add-ons, off by
default: [Lelap's own add-ons](../brief.md#decisions) and [Open lectures](../brief.md#decisions) in
the brief.

**Where it runs: on the catalog service** (`services/catalog/src/lectures/`,
`/lectures/manifest.json`, `app.lelap.lectures`). The list is the same for everyone, and the
service fetches the transcripts from Yale's pages, which it may: they are text. Audio goes from
Yale or archive.org straight to the listener, never through the service.

## What the sources offer

Checked on 2026-11-19.

- **Licence and credit.** Yale's terms put most lectures and course material under
  [CC BY-NC-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/us/); third-party content is
  not covered unless a lecture's Credits section says so. Any use must credit the lecturer and
  Yale in a set form, “[Name], [Course Title] (Yale University: Open Yale Courses),
  http://oyc.yale.edu. License: Creative Commons BY-NC-SA”, and link the terms
  ([Open Yale Courses terms, sections 4 to 6](https://oyc.yale.edu/terms)). The terms' non-commercial
  section rules out fees and advertising; Lelap has neither.
- **archive.org copies.** The `openyalecourses` collection holds 47 items: 42 audio courses and 5
  videos ([search](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php?q=collection%3Aopenyalecourses&fl%5B%5D=identifier&fl%5B%5D=mediatype&fl%5B%5D=licenseurl&rows=100&output=json)).
  They were uploaded as copies, and 46 of the 47 have no `licenseurl`, so the Internet Archive
  add-on's rule would leave them out; their licence is Yale's. `oyc_Roman_Architecture` has 24
  MP3s, one per lecture, with lengths in its file list
  ([metadata](https://archive.org/metadata/oyc_Roman_Architecture)).
- **Yale's own pages still link MP3s.** Each lecture page has the YouTube embed, the transcript and
  an Audio link. Lecture 1 of nine courses (HSAR 252, PSYC 110, AFAM 162, ECON 159, PHYS 200,
  HIST 116, ENGL 310, CHEM 125a, PHIL 176) links an MP3 under
  `oyc.yale.edu/sites/default/files/courses/…`, and each answered `200 audio/mpeg`; so did the old
  `openmedia.yale.edu` address for HSAR 252 (for example
  [HSAR 252, lecture 1](https://oyc.yale.edu/history-art/hsar-252/lecture-1)). This differs from
  what the maintainer found (a host answering 404); see the first step below.
- **Transcripts** are on each lecture page as text, under the same licence. Yale's `robots.txt`
  doesn't exclude lecture pages ([robots.txt](https://oyc.yale.edu/robots.txt)).

## Plan

- **Settle the audio host first.** Sample every listed course: does each lecture's Yale MP3 answer,
  and does its archive.org copy match it in count and length? Write the result here. Then, per
  course, one stream: Yale's own files where they all answer (the publisher's copy, with its
  licence), archive.org's where they don't. One stream per course, not both, since places are kept
  by the stream's `sourceKey`.
- **The hand-kept list.** `services/catalog/src/lectures/courses.ts`: per course the Yale code,
  title, lecturer, term, Yale course page, archive.org identifier and the lectures in order, each
  with its title, Yale page, audio address and length. Built once by a script in `scripts/` from
  the Yale pages and archive.org's metadata, then read by hand: only courses whose every lecture
  has audio, and no lecture whose Credits section puts the recording outside the licence. The
  service never discovers courses at runtime.
- **Books with parts.** `audiobook` items with `parts` (one per lecture, with its title and
  length); the lecturer as author, the course page as `link`, the department as genre. A catalog
  “Open Yale Courses” with `genre` and `search` over the list.
- **Credit and licence on every course.** Each course's description ends with Yale's credit line
  in its own form, the licence's name and the terms' address. Its `link` opens the Yale course
  page.
- **Transcripts.** A protocol addition (revision 12): `transcripts` on a stream part, shaped like a
  feed's `podcast:transcript` (address, type, language). `StreamPart` is a plain zod object, so an
  older app drops the field and still plays. The app opens its transcript screen (M23's, for
  episodes) for a book part that has one. The service answers
  `/lectures/transcript/{course}/{lecture}.txt`: the text from Yale's lecture page with the credit
  line on top, UTF-8 plain text, cached for a month through `upstream.json`.
- **No video.** The YouTube embeds and the five video items are never used or linked; a course
  that exists only as video is not on the list.
- **Later courses.** Other universities' complete courses can join the list on the same terms: the
  publisher states an open licence, and the audio is on the publisher's host or archive.org.
- **Offered, off by default**, with Add under Lelap's own add-ons, as Lepak is
  ([M131](m131.md)). It overlaps the Internet Archive add-on's Lectures and talks row, which
  stays as it is.
- **Tests.** The list against a schema (every lecture has audio and a length); a recorded Yale page
  for the transcript extraction; the description ends with the credit line.

## Done when

- With Open lectures added, Roman Architecture lists as one book with 24 parts in order and plays
  from part 1 to part 24.
- Every course's page shows Yale's credit line and the licence, and links Yale's course page.
- During a lecture, the transcript screen shows that lecture's text, credit line first.
- No course on the list depends on YouTube.
