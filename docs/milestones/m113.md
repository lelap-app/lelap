# M113 Big screens 2

[M44](m44.md) gave tablets and laptops a rail and two panes. M113 builds on it for foldables,
tablets and the desktop web app. The decision is in the brief under
[Big screens](../brief.md#decisions).

- **Columns:** `columnsFor` in `packages/core/src/screen.ts` gives a window one column (phones),
  two (from 720 × 480 points: the list and the player) or three (from 1400 points wide). With
  three, a book's or a show's page opens in a middle column between the list it came from and the
  player (`openPage` in `apps/app/src/navigation.ts`, `DetailPane`), with ✕ to close it. When the
  window narrows, the page moves to full screen. Item and podcast pages live in
  `ItemPage.tsx` and `PodcastPage.tsx`; the routes are thin wrappers.
- **Resizable panes:** the line before the player and before the page can be dragged, or moved with
  ← and → once focused (Home goes back to the default); `Divider.tsx`. `paneWidths` keeps the
  widths within limits and narrows the page, then the player, so the list keeps 320 points. The
  widths are saved per profile on the device (`playerPaneWidth`, `detailPaneWidth`), not synced.
- **Foldables:** the window size can't tell a half-open book from a flat one or where the hinge is,
  so `modules/fold` reads the hinge through Jetpack WindowManager (`window-java` 1.3.0) and
  `foldLayout` decides: the screens left of a book's hinge and the player right of it, or the
  screens above a tabletop's hinge and the player below, nothing drawn under the hinge. A flat,
  seamless fold keeps the normal layout. Below a narrow tabletop's hinge (a flip phone) the player
  leaves out the cover. `Folds` is tested on the JVM.
- **Drag to a list:** on the web the rail lists the profile's lists under the tabs; a book row
  (Library, lists, series) or an episode row can be dragged onto a hand-made list with the
  browser's own drag and drop (`apps/app/src/drag.web.ts`, `RailLists.tsx`). The list lights up
  and says Added. Smart lists take no drop.
- **No stretched screens:** list screens keep to 960 points in the middle (`LIST_COLUMN`: Library,
  Search, Podcasts, a list, Downloads, Up Next, Convert, Match) and forms to 720 (`READABLE`:
  Add-ons, Profiles, a profile, Sync, Server, Scrobbler, Stats, Year, Transcript). Checked in the
  web app at 320, 768, 1280 and 1920 points.

Left for [M120](m120.md): drag to a list on Android, from catalog tiles, and onto the Library's list
cards.

## Open checks

- [ ] (adb) [fold] On a foldable emulator (Pixel Fold or “7.6" Fold-in”), open flat: the normal
      rail and two panes. Half open like a book (`adb shell cmd device_state state 1` or the
      emulator's posture control): the screens end at the hinge, the player starts after it, and no
      button sits on the hinge.
- [ ] (adb) [fold] The same foldable turned sideways and half open on a table: the screens above the
      hinge with the tabs just above it, the player below with the cover beside the controls.
- [ ] (adb) [fold] A flip-style emulator half open on a table: the player below the hinge without a
      cover; nothing loaded shows “Nothing is playing.”; opening it flat gives the mini player back.
- [ ] (adb) [fold] Fold and unfold while a book plays: the layout follows each time, also after the
      app was in the background, and playback carries on.
- [ ] (adb) A 10-inch tablet sideways: drag the line before the player with a finger; it follows,
      stops at its limits, and the width is still there after closing and opening the app.
- [ ] (adb) The same tablet with TalkBack: the line reads “Player width”, swiping up and down
      makes the player wider and narrower.
- [ ] (adb) A tablet in landscape at 1400 dp wide or more (or a desktop-size emulator): a book from
      Home opens in the middle column; ✕ closes it, and so does Back without leaving Home;
      turning the tablet upright moves the page to full screen.
- [ ] (adb) [fold] On a foldable half open, open car mode and the full-screen player (from the
      notification): each covers the whole screen, across the hinge.
- [ ] (you) In Chrome, Firefox and Safari at 1920 px: drag a book from the Library and an episode
      from a show's page onto a list in the rail with the mouse; the list lights up while over it,
      says Added, and has the item. A smart list doesn't light up. Dragging a file from the desktop
      onto a list does nothing.
- [ ] (you) In a browser at 1920 px, subscribe to a podcast from the Podcasts tab: the show's page
      opens in the middle column; dragging the line before it and before the player works with the
      mouse, and ←, → and Home move it once it has focus.
