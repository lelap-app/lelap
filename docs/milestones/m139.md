# M139 Casting 6

What [M125](m125.md) left. Decision in the [brief](../brief.md#decisions) (“Speaker groups”).

- **Waits tuned from measured timings** (`modules/cast`: `ReceiverWaits.kt`, `RemoteSession.kt`,
  `FCast.kt`). No test round has written receiver timings down yet (M69's last one logged none),
  so instead of new fixed values each receiver tunes its own: `RemoteSession` already measures
  how long it takes to agree with a play, pause, seek or load, and `ReceiverWaits` turns its
  slower answers (the 90th percentile of the last 20, half again, plus a poll) into the waits
  after a command (from 2.5 s, between 1.5 and 8 s) and after a load (from 5 s, between 2.5 and
  15 s). The defaults hold until a receiver answered three times. Each timing line in
  `adb logcat -s LelapCast` now ends with the waits in use. The 10 s before a part's end that
  still count as its end stay as they are: a receiver that stopped too early can't be told from
  one that was stopped, so nothing could be learnt there. FCast's watch learns the gaps between a
  receiver's pongs: one that answers slower than every 3 s gets a longer grace for the place
  (half again its slower gaps) and longer before it counts as lost (four gaps, up to 30 s), never
  shorter than before; a gap across the phone sleeping doesn't count. A closing FCast session
  logs `fcast pong gap … ms`.
- **The leading speaker leaves** (`Group.kt`, `CastLink.kt`, `CastSheet.tsx`). Decided: the
  speaker that joined first after it leads from there, so the music doesn't stop for the
  others. `GroupRenderer.handOver` lets the new leader's thread run what was queued for it, then
  calls it on the cast thread, so nothing it was told is reordered; the old leader stops at the
  volume it had before a fade and is let go. The new leader keeps the file and speed the group
  plays, so a speaker that joins later still loads the right part. The cast sheet offers Take
  out on every speaker of a group, and a leader that drops off the network hands over the same
  way; alone, it still ends the session. A speaker still loading after it joined can't lead;
  who leads is decided on the cast thread, so a double tap or a loss at the same moment takes
  out only the one speaker.

## Verified here

- JVM: `ReceiverWaitsTest` (defaults, quick and slow speakers, bounds, part ends ignored),
  `FCastTest` (slow pongs, quick receivers keep the defaults), `GroupRendererTest` (hand-over,
  queued calls before it, a leader alone, a speaker still joining), `FCastTest` (a pong after
  the phone slept).
