# M122 Better sound 3

What [M101](m101.md) left out. Decision in the [brief](../brief.md#decisions) (“Sound effects”).

- **More formats** (`packages/core/src/webTracks.ts`, Vitest in `webTracks.test.ts`): skip
  silence finds where a moment lies in M4B and other MP4 files with AAC (the sample tables, with
  the edit list's start), Ogg Opus and Vorbis (the granule position at the end of each page) and
  FLAC (the sample number in each frame header, checked by its CRC-8). Constant-bitrate MP3 keeps
  M101's even layout, decoded with `decodeAudioData` as before; the rest are decoded packet by
  packet with WebCodecs' `AudioDecoder` (`apps/app/src/platform/websilence.ts`), and the sound is
  placed by where its last packet ends. Variable-bitrate MP3 is left out: its frames are counted
  right, but Chrome's own time after a seek doesn't follow them (measured below; [M134](m134.md)). Ogg and FLAC have no table, so a chunk's bytes are guessed from the
  places earlier chunks found (`ByteClock`), a page or frame early. A browser without WebCodecs,
  or without the codec, plays the book unchanged. An M4B's tables (3.3 MB for 3 h 39 min) are
  read only once skip silence is on.
- **Read once** (`apps/app/web/sw.js`, `apps/app/src/platform/webtee.ts`): the service worker
  passes on to the page what the media element streams, piece by piece as the element reads it,
  for the file skip silence asked about, and skip silence decodes those bytes. The element gets
  the same answer; when it stops reading (a seek), the download stops too. A host that hides
  `Content-Range` from scripts, as archive.org does, gets it back from the range asked for and
  `Content-Length`. Bytes are kept in memory only, at most 32 MB per file, and let go once the
  place is a megabyte past them. What the element never reads (just before a seek's place) is
  fetched with a range request, as is what it hasn't fetched within 2 s, a part where skip
  silence was switched on while it played, and everything without the service worker (a dev
  build). Skip silence now makes a non-routed element ask with CORS, which such a host allows.
- **Cast receivers** play without the effects still: Cast's default receiver, UPnP and FCast
  take a file, a speed and a volume, nothing more (`GoogleCast.kt`, `Upnp.kt`, `FCast.kt`). Skip
  silence through them would mean the phone switching the receiver's speed at each pause's edge
  over the network, with a place it only learns from status replies; that wasn't tried. A stream the phone renders with its effects is
  planned in [M134](m134.md).
- The sound sheet's web note names the formats.

## Verified here

In the exported web app with its service worker, in headless Chrome 158 on macOS, playing 60 s
from 1:00 with skip silence on. The pause map was compared with a decode of the whole file by
ffmpeg under the same rules, and the sound playing (the analyser after the effects, read every
5 ms) with the element's time.

| File                                                                 | Map against ffmpeg    | Played in 60 s | Fast while loud | Read by skip silence     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------- | --------------- | ------------------------ |
| LibriVox The Time Machine 1, 64 kbit/s MP3 (archive.org), from 10:00 | 29 of 29 within 10 ms | 67.4 s         | 0 ms            | 874 kB shared, 0 own     |
| The same, served locally                                             | 29 of 29              | 67.7 s         | 0 ms            | 808 kB shared, 64 kB own |
| LibriVox The Time Machine, M4B (AAC, 3 h 39 min), local              | 28 of 28              | 67.9 s         | 0 ms            | 4.2 MB shared, 0 own     |
| LibriVox The Time Machine 1, Ogg Vorbis (archive.org) ¹              | 28 of 28              | 67.3 s         | 0 ms            | 1.9 MB shared, 13 kB own |
| The same, served locally                                             | 28 of 28              | 67.6 s         | 0 ms            | 1.9 MB shared, 0 own     |
| Hacker Public Radio 4035, Opus, local                                | 19 of 19              | 62.8 s         | 0 ms            | 1.6 MB shared, 0 own     |
| The same episode, FLAC (192 kHz), local                              | 20 of 20              | 62.8 s         | 0 ms            | 7.5 MB shared, 61 kB own |

- Every fast stretch began 152 to 243 ms into its pause and ended 132 to 259 ms before the voice
  came back (by the element's time). No stretch of the map was missing in the minute played.
- The sound went quiet 12 to 16 ms (median) after the map's pause start for the WebCodecs
  formats, and 47 to 67 ms for MP3 (see [M134](m134.md) on MP3's encoder delay).
- “Shared” is what came from the element's stream through the service worker; “own” what skip
  silence fetched itself: the file's first 64 kB before the element's first piece arrived, or
  bytes just before the place the element jumped to. M4B's 4.2 MB includes its 3.3 MB tables.
- The first chunk was mapped 7 to 91 ms after Play. archive.org sends no CORS headers for
  LibriVox's M4B, so that one played unchanged from there. ¹ Measured on this branch before the review fixes (headers out of Ogg runs, less waiting for the stream).
- A variable-bitrate MP3 (LAME `-V 6` of part 1): the map matched ffmpeg (27 of 27), but from 1:00
  the sound was loud during 8.4 s of the 10.6 s played fast, and from 15:00 during 3.9 s of 6.3 s.
  So these files are left alone; after the change, the same file plays unchanged.
- In the app itself: LibriVox's The Art of War (Moira Fogarty) from the catalog, skip silence
  switched on in the sound sheet, part 2 for 60 s: 61.5 s played, 404 kB shared, 0 own.
- Vitest: `webTracks.test.ts` (MP4 tables and edit list, Ogg pages, CRC and continued packets,
  headers left out, Vorbis description, FLAC frames and a false sync, VBR frame counting,
  `ByteClock`), `webSound.test.ts`.
