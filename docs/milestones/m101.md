# M101 Better sound 2

What [M84](m84.md) left out. Decision in the [brief](../brief.md#decisions) (“Sound effects”).

- **Skip silence on the web** (`apps/app/src/platform/websilence.ts`): a media element can't drop
  frames, so the player reads the file 30 s ahead of the place in 10 s chunks, decodes them with
  `decodeAudioData` and marks 10 ms windows whose peak stays under -30 dBFS (Media3's level). The
  middle of each pause over 0.4 s plays four times faster; 150 ms at each end stay at the
  listening speed, so no word is sped up. A pause lasts about what Android keeps of it: half, at
  most 0.75 s, as far as four times allows. The element's time stays the file's time, so the place
  is always right. Constant-bitrate MP3s only, such as LibriVox's: their bytes map evenly onto
  time (`mp3Layout`, with the LAME encoder delay). Bytes come straight from the audio host with
  range requests, or from the download in OPFS; never through a Lelap service. The time saved is
  kept per book in `localStorage` and shown in the sheet as on Android. Pure logic, with Vitest:
  `packages/core/src/webSilence.ts`.
- **Even loudness on the web** (`webaudio.ts`): a gain after voice boost, before volume boost,
  with the limiter on. ReplayGain from the file's first bytes (ID3v2 `TXXX`, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis
  comments, Opus R128, MP4 freeform atoms when `moov` comes first; `webLoudness.ts`); otherwise an
  `AnalyserNode` reads a 400 ms block every 400 ms, and the gain glides towards -20 dBFS within
  ±10 dB, with the Android `Leveller`'s numbers. A new book is measured afresh.
- **Steadier accounting on Android** (`SoundProcessor.kt`, `SilenceSavings.kt`): skipped silence
  is counted per book on the playback thread (`SilenceLedger`). Media3 hands each flush the
  stream's period, so the flush that starts another book's stream first counts the old one's
  tail, then switches; a book switch only banks what was counted. ReplayGain is read from each
  part's format as the renderer configures the sink (`SoundAudioSink`, `StreamGains`) and applied
  at the flush that starts that part, so a part's first moment no longer plays at the previous
  part's gain. A new book's loudness starts afresh at the same flush.
- **Cast receivers** still play without the effects: no protocol Lelap casts with offers them.

What is left is in [M122](m122.md).

## Verified here

In the web preview (Chromium), with LibriVox's The Time Machine, part 1 (64 kbit/s MPEG 2
MP3, 19 min 32 s), streamed from archive.org:

- The pause map the player built from 10 s chunks matched a decode of the whole file: all 15
  pauses between 15:20 and 16:55 started at the same 10 ms window and ended within one.
- Over 90 s of playing with skip silence, 94.7 s of the file played: 14 pauses sped up, each fast
  stretch starting 168 to 237 ms after its pause began and ending 140 to 246 ms before it ended.
  The sheet's counter grew by 4.2 s.
- For the whole file, the same rules find 169 pauses (2 min 25 s) and save 67.2 s (5.7 %);
  Android's halving of the same pauses would save 76.1 s.
- The element's time and the decoded file agreed within about 30 ms, also after a seek.
- Even loudness: The Time Machine measured -24.8 dBFS and got +4.7 dB; The Art of War (Moira
  Fogarty) measured -30.0 dBFS and got +10 dB, the most it lifts. Before, 5.2 dB apart; after,
  both at -20 dBFS. The gain reached its target within 10 s.
- Kotlin JVM tests: the ledger's accounting per book, gains per part, ReplayGain from Media3
  metadata (`BetterSoundTest.kt`). Vitest: `webSound.test.ts`.
