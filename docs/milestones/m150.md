# M150 Better sound 5

What [M134](m134.md) left out. Measured in Firefox 147 and Chrome 158, headless on macOS, with
M134's harness rebuilt (in this group's session notes, not in the repository). Decision in the
[brief](../brief.md#decisions) (“Sound effects”).

## What shipped

- **Variable-bitrate MP3 in Firefox** (`firefoxXingSeek` and `vbrMp3Track(…, 'firefox')` in
  `packages/core/src/webTracks.ts`, `mp3SeekRule`). After a seek Firefox was 0.05 to 1.9 s away
  from the frames' own times. Its rule, fitted to 74 seeks in six LAME files (`-V 2` to `-V 9`,
  22.05 to 48 kHz, one with a 200 kB ID3 tag): the table of contents' place for the seek's whole
  hundredth, not interpolated and counted from the file's first byte, so an ID3 tag moves it; a
  frame index from the Xing header's bytes and frames in 32-bit floats; when that index is past
  the seek, the same again from 1 % earlier; play from the first frame at or after the index's
  byte, which gets the index's time. 73 seeks within 15 ms, one a frame (26 ms) off; skip
  silence's fast stretches keep 130 ms from the voice. Skip silence now runs on these files in
  Firefox.
- **Constant-bitrate MP3 in Firefox** (`firefoxGuessesMp3` in `webSilence.ts`). Without an Info
  frame Firefox estimates frames from the average of those it has read, which differs from the
  file's where frames vary by a padding byte (44.1 and 22.05 kHz): 1.4 s off at 5 minutes, 4.2 s
  at 15. Skip silence used to speed through words there; such files now play unchanged in
  Firefox. With an Info frame, or at 48 kHz, Firefox stays a frame ahead.
- **Where a CBR seek lands, measured, nothing moved.** Chrome plays one frame (25 ms) behind its
  time at 44.1 kHz until 10 minutes in, then on time; 54 to 75 ms at 22.05 kHz. Firefox one frame
  ahead. All inside the 130 to 150 ms the fast stretches keep from a pause's edges, so the map
  isn't placed per browser.
- **Time saved while casting** (`CastSkipTally`, `RenderClock.skippedMs`). While the phone renders
  a part for a receiver, the part's time skip silence left out of what the receiver has played
  goes into the book's time saved, at the stream's speed, each moment once.
- **Joining a rendered group** (`CastLink.catchUpLive`, `LiveJoin`, `RemotePlayer.joinStream`). A
  speaker that joins while the phone renders gets a stream of its own from the leader's place, so
  the others play on; when its load took over a second, a second stream starts that much further
  ahead. The media server keeps the leader's stream open for it. Without a place to start from,
  the group starts again together as before.

Tests: `webTracks.test.ts` (Firefox's seek, the 1 % rewind, an ID3 tag, landing on the next
frame, `mp3SeekRule`), `webSound.test.ts` (`firefoxGuessesMp3`); Kotlin `RenderClockTest`
(skipped time, the join's place and second lead), `CastSkipTallyTest`.
